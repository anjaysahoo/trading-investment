Doing nothing; narratives; two books

After this: sit out without FOMO, exit only on your own signals, and keep the investor book and the trader book apart.

Catch the rally by dodging the sell-off

"The best way to catch a rally is to have dodged the sell-off." You control only your positioning, not reclaims or rallies. During a 4-month −60% downtrend he made no redundant bullish arguments, so he wasn't traumatized when the reclaim came. "Being early is being wrong": an alt-season thesis that is down 60% is no "I told you so".

99% of the time: waiting

Out of a position, you wait for a reclaim, strength, a HH or a daily structure shift. In one, you wait for invalidation or TP. There are no real forecasters: signals "fall into our lap". Set red flags (break below the 4H 200s, a range forms, a break below the range) and do something else until one fires. "I want to be as lazy… as possible."

Exit on the system you entered on

Don't enter on the 4H 200s / HTF trend / structure and then exit on a liquidity sweep, an RSI bear div or high OI ("people want to be long in a bull market… who would have thought"). Accept your system's good, bad and ugly: a 4H 200s invalidation 20% below a +50% rip is slow, and you knew that going in, so handle it with TPs at key regions. De-risking for election week is cope: you knew it was coming.

In an uptrend, know what not to do

Trend lines are subjective. "How do you know that your trend lines are alpha?" Wicky "top" candles print all through a bull market, and 3/4 to 4/5 of them are false positives. RSI bear divs in an uptrend were wrong 7 times, then "right" 3 months and +40% later. "Turn the RSI off." Bet on the 20-0 boxer: he loses eventually, but after 40 wins it doesn't matter. The edge is the discretion to know what not to do.

Narratives follow price

"Show me the charts, I'll tell you the news." On the way down it was "FUD is priced in", "Fed pivot", "merge supply shock". On the way up came the bearish merge takes. "It's so easy to fall victim to a narrative." Let the chart tell you which narrative to abide by. He'd believe "a koala stuck in a tree… is bullish" if price agrees. Use narratives only as confluence. The TA is "the equivalent of crayons, and it works".

Two books, never mixed

He keeps pre-positioned spot "just sitting there" in a Ledger. He DCA'd it 2017 → Q1 2022, never took profit, and ate the 2022 drawdown as an accepted con. The trading account runs the system. Net worth hit an ATH despite −80% on the investor bucket. Mix them and you land in limbo: "every time the market goes up you're a shitty investor… every time it goes down you're a shitty trader." "Once you start to mix the two… you quickly start to fall into a downward spiral."

Nurses with a 401k out-trade you

The greatest investors are labor: 401k/403b DCA, where "their main variable is time" and the entry price matters less over time. If your 2–3-year return is below theirs (~30%), "you got out traded by someone who doesn't even know what… they're doing". It takes humility to admit you'd sometimes be better off doing nothing.

The bi-weekly cadence is the art of doing nothing, built in. Investor book: a recurring DCA into cold storage that never gets reviewed for exits. Trader book: on review day only, check the red flags (4H 200s lost, range formed, range lost) and move limit orders. Between reviews, no chart-checking and no exits on news, RSI or narratives. If no red flag fired and no order filled, the correct action is none.

If → then

  • Downtrend → no redundant bullish arguments, sit in cash, wait for the reclaim.
  • No signal → do nothing. Red flag (4H 200s lost / range / range lost) → act.
  • Exit reason must match the entry system: no RSI, candle, OI, sweep, news or election exits.
  • Uptrend → ignore trend lines, top candles and bear divs.
  • Narrative vs chart → the chart wins; narratives are confluence only.
  • Investor book = DCA, untouched. Trader book = system. Know which one you're acting in.
  • Chop → ease off the gas.
