After this: put the stop where your reasons die, and always know which plan (A, B, A2, C) you're in.

Justification, expectation, invalidation

Know the three "-ations" and "forget about the fourth one, which is liquidation". "Your justification for why you're in a trade is also the exact same reason as to why you would exit the trade." It works with any system (Ichimoku, Elliott, anything) as long as you know these three.

Scaled stop: double the R, lower the odds

The textbook stop "killed your RR". A scaled stop hides below the mini structure + 30m 200s + demand ("if those break I'm mostly invalid"). It can "quite literally double up your RR", but probabilities skew slightly against you. Know that you did it. With no micro structure to hide under, it's just arbitrary. The textbook version here was 2.5R: "I probably don't even take that trade."

Your edge is knowing when not to use your system

"No indicator is actually profitable." Shorting every RSI bear divergence loses. The edge is spotting which one is different. "Discretion is the ability to know when not to apply something", and algorithms can't turn themselves off. Knowing when not implies knowing when to. The reverse isn't true.

Plan A → B → A2 → C

Plan A is the thesis (e.g. trend along the 200s). Plan B is when A is invalid: "it's not that we're bearish now, it's that this trend is bearish", so expect a deeper pullback. Plan A2 is a reclaim of "the exact same thing that has allowed for this profit": re-enter with the stop below the MAs + recent structure + local demand. If A2 fails, don't go back to B. That's Plan C: "totally reassess". Three bias shifts in three days (~2% each) means the environment changed, so hide the MAs and think like a range trader (Ch 4).

Scaled stops need someone watching the 30m, so they are not-biweekly. Use the textbook stop: below daily structure + 4H 200s + the zone. If the textbook R to the next level is small (he skips ~2.5R), skip the trade. At each review, write down which plan you're in:

  • A: above the 4H 200s, structure intact → leave everything.
  • B: lost the 4H 200s → don't add. The stop stays where the thesis dies.
  • A2: reclaimed → place a new bid at the reclaimed 200s, stop below MAs + structure + demand.
  • C: 3+ bias flips since last review → cancel trend bids. Range rules or nothing.

Textbook vs scaled: no fixed rule. He prefers the scaled stop and accepts the wicks, yet the same video shows the textbook stop surviving ▶ Stop Loss 6:01. The RR+Prob video says to balance and not chase R, while this one skips a 2.5R textbook trade.

Timeframes drift up. The plan and stop examples (Jun 2023, Feb 2024) run on 15m/30m trends ▶ Giving Back 14:04. The trade-management video (Mar 2024) works top-down from 4H/daily ▶ Manage Trades 0:30.

If → then

  • Stop = where every entry reason has flipped. Not before.
  • Textbook R ≈ 2.5 or less → probably skip.
  • Tighter stop than textbook → you bought R with probability. Know it.
  • Stopped on the LTF, HTF still up → same setup on the next TF (4H 200s + daily).
  • Plan A invalid → B (deeper pullback). Reclaim → A2. A2 fails → C, never back to B.
  • 3 bias flips in ~3 days → Plan C: range mindset, act only on the break.
