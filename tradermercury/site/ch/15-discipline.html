Learn yourself first

Markets are "more about trying to master yourself." His flaw was a passive nature: "as soon as that position went in my favor I would see green and my monkey brain would just immediately want to market close." Winners got cut small, losers ran full size. Aggressive types hit other walls, like blown accounts. Pick the timeframe that fits your nature (HTF suits passive). "This is not basketball": the market never forces you to play your weaknesses.

The domino effect

The trigger is a fear that isn't tangible: "the fear is that we're going to uptrend and you're going to be left on the sideline." So you buy back full spot. Then "bitcoinlover69420 tweets", you capitulate, and you end up net short, fading your own thesis. "One decision based off of an impulse leads to another… better off if I just held on to one position." A boredom trade to "pay for a steak dinner" does the same: down 20%, or stuck in a stale position when the real setup comes. See domino effect.

Fear of being late

Skip a 13–15% move, find arguments (bear div, overextended, a sweep), and miss the next 66%. Then buy the top, get stopped, and swear off the next move. The cycle repeats, and you live in limbo. "The universe hates you": the trend breaks the one time you finally join. Had you traded the 17 times before, you'd be up anyway.

Overtrading is the downfall

"I've taken two trades from January to May… might have outperformed 99% of you." In Q1 2023 there were "only three moves you need to catch": "I didn't overtrade, I didn't overcomplicate it." One action a month is plenty. Chop is "not why you're here". Get your dopamine elsewhere: a show, games, a hobby, a bike ride. "Make sure that every button press you make has a lot of thought put behind it."

Limit the amplitude, not the emotions

Emotions come in waves on every timeframe, from the morning-coffee spike to a breakup. You are not a robot: robots win in trends but "get chopped the absolute hell up" in ranges. Cap the peaks and troughs instead. HTF isn't "easier". It just has fewer waves, "less sample size to [mess] yourself over on". Convenience works against you: checking every 5 minutes means you can "capitulate your entire portfolio… within two seconds." Obsession pushes success away. "Limit the emotional bias for whatever happens next."

The edge is knowing when not to play

"Being a trader means you wait for things to fall into your lap… this is not poker… no ante." Sitting out costs nothing, and "no one's going to punish your weaknesses". The only way to pay to sit is to trade the chop. See no ante.

Mitigate human error

Real mistakes he has made: a Bonk order missing a zero turned into a market buy, an extra zero on size, and month-old forgotten limit orders that filled. Overconfidence breeds these errors. His rule: "I will not trade if I'm tired… if I can't keep my eyes open, I'm not allowed to go put on the trade."

Journal the hold times

One sub-account: 10 trades, 7 wins, 3 losses. Winners were held about 2 weeks, losers under 12 hours. "When I'm wrong I should know… within a relatively short period." So he cuts faster in the first 12h to 1 day if the trade isn't working. Your journal shows your own version of this pattern.

Trade it like it's Tesla

BTC +7–8% floods his DMs. BCH +100% or Tesla +40% gets crickets. When crypto becomes your personality, the pressure to outperform makes you easier to crack. Trade an asset as if you had no attachment to it, and you stay objective and fluid. The right investor/trader balance is personal: income, expenses, environment. "I was you and I hated it."

Take the loss on the chin

Buy range lows, and if they don't hold: "you take your loss… you cry about it for five minutes, you get over it." Below the range, expect further expansion, not a reason to average down. Regret is infinite anyway: buy the exact bottom and you'll regret not using leverage. "There's always something to pout about."

Bi-weekly version: the cadence is the discipline. Buttons get pressed on review day only, so there are no boredom or 2am trades. On review day: run the four-box checklist on every order, cancel every limit order that no longer matches a live setup (pre-placed orders are exactly what forgotten fills are made of), and log each closed trade's hold time. If a new position is under water by the first review and the reason for entry is gone, cut it. If you're tired, move the review. Don't rush it.

If → then

  • Impulse trade just taken → no second trade until the next review.
  • Bored / chop → no trade; get dopamine elsewhere.
  • Tired / 2am → no order.
  • Any order → check price zeros, size zeros, stale limit orders.
  • Skipped a move → don't chase the top; wait for the next setup.
  • Loser not working within ~1 day (or one review) and the reason is gone → cut.
  • Stopped out → cry 5 minutes, move on. No averaging down below the range.
  • Feeling pressure from an asset you love → size and trade it like one you don't care about.
