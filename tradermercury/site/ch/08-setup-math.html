Three aspects of every trade

Risk-reward, perceived probability, and your personal situation. The first two make a setup. The third is "the most important" and the one a setup ignores. It applies to trading and investing alike.

You only ever perceive probability

In blackjack a hard 16 against a face card has a known answer: hit. "We don't have that luxury in trading", so we perceive the odds from price action. You skew them with structure: to downtrend on a higher timeframe, price must downtrend on a lower one first (the snowball).

Your situation can flip the same trade

With 90% of net worth already exposed, an extra 12R low-probability reversal does little. If you're under-exposed or net-negative, the same trade works as a "long hedge" and may be worth it. Personal situation goes deep: income, savings, what you can afford to lose, family and mortgage vs risk-aggressive. "If you master this one… drawing the setups becomes second nature."

Stop taking high-probability/low-RR trades, and stop taking high-RR/low-probability ones. "Find setups that I perceive to be decently probable with solid risk reward." Size from the stop

risk $ = account × 1%, and position = risk $ ÷ stop % (as a decimal). His numbers: a $10,000 account risks $100. With a 2% stop the position is 100 ÷ 0.02 = $5,000. With a 10% stop it's $1,000. A wider stop means a smaller position, and the loss stays the same either way. "Don't ever risk more than what you need to risk."

If → then

  • Position = (account × risk %) ÷ stop %. Default risk 1%.
  • Wider stop → smaller position, never bigger risk.
  • High R, low probability (bottom-catch) → skip. Look for decent probability with solid R.
  • Already heavily exposed → an extra speculative long adds little. Pass.
  • ~50% win rate with ~6R winners → take the setup every time it appears.
  • Beginner → static risk. Size up only on A+ confluence, and only later.
