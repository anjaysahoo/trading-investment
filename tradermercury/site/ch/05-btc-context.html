HTF context first

Start from BTC's HTF environment. The daily 200 EMA and 200 MA are "a foundation to stack every single lower-timeframe analysis on top of". Context doesn't predict anything. It tells you where the key inflection points are, in both directions.

Speculation vs interpretation

Draw random lines with price hidden, then turn price on, and your brain will find "tests" of them. So anything you draw is speculation, his gray boxes included. Interpretation is data everyone sees the same way: structure, MAs, volume. Even then, the same data reads as a rabbit to one person and a duck to another. That disagreement is why markets exist.

Valid vs actionable

"Price can go 38% higher and still be bearish." A weekly downtrend thesis is "equally valid" at the bottom or 30% up, so there's no action in it. If a thesis needs a 30–60% move to be proven wrong, it's valid but not actionable. Pinpoint a closer level instead, one where losing it means "lights out… you're not buying a dip, you're buying a downtrend". "Weekly still bullish" is hope.

Relative strength: BTC gates, outperformers pay

Read BTC first. When equities rise and BTC doesn't, BTC is weaker. When equities fall and BTC doesn't, it's lagging, and it eventually follows. Then measure the payoff of the same setup across assets: from one range breakout, BTC gave "a measly 15%" while ETH gave 60%. He skipped the BTC breakout and traded the alts that were outperforming instead (RNDR went from $4 to $12 after reclaiming its 4H 200s).

Recency bias

A −6% CPI-day drop felt massive only because the last 3 weeks had traded a ~5–9% range, while BTC's average daily range is ~5%. Recency bias means "attributing a higher degree of significance to something that was more recently said or occurred". Measure a move against the ADR and the bigger range, not the last few weeks. He has never traded a CPI day: "it's predatory, it's out to get you."

Think in shades, not extremes

Bullish doesn't mean 250k and bearish doesn't mean 10k. It's rare for every timeframe to line up (the 2022 bear was one of those times). About 90% of the time the view is contextual: bullish on this timeframe, bearish on that one.

If → then

  • Always read BTC's HTF (200D, range) before any alt.
  • The thesis needs a 30–60% move to be invalidated → not actionable. Find the nearer level or proxy.
  • You drew it → it's speculation. Only act on it when interpretation (structure, 200s) agrees.
  • Equities ↑ and BTC flat → weaker. Equities ↓ and BTC flat → lagging.
  • The same setup pays more on one asset → trade the outperformer.
  • A move feels huge → compare it to the ADR and the bigger range. Skip CPI/event days.
