Reading the cycle

After this: name any drop as a dip, a pullback or a downtrend, and know the only place worth buying a bottom.

One ladder of trends

Each set of 200s portrays the next timeframe's structure: 1H 200s ≈ 4H structure, 4H 200s ≈ daily, 200D ≈ weekly. MAs lag. Waiting for the 200D alone is "having fun doing nothing and missing out on the rally". When every rung is up, context is easy: buy dips along the local trend.

Dip, pullback or downtrend?

Label the drop by which trend it lost. A dip wicks into the local trend (1H 200s, ~1–3%) and the trend resumes. If the local trend breaks, you are "now buying a pullback" into the HTF trend (4H 200s). If the HTF trend is lost and the daily makes a lower low, it is a downtrend. "It's all semantics", but clear names let you digest it to your gut.

A snowman needs a snowball first

"If you wanted to create a snowman you'd have to build a snowball first." Price can't test the 4H 200s without first losing the local trend. The recovery runs the same chain in reverse: reclaim 1H 200s → reclaim 4H 200s → daily higher high.

Act only at inflection points

An inflection point is where assumptions scale either way: hold → more bullish, lose → significantly bearish. A plain support is not one. "I'm a trend trader, not a support level trader." Price reached this support by breaking several others. Is this one more significant? "My honest answer is I don't [know]."

Downtrends don't behave like dips

Buy a downtrend expecting dip behavior and you get different results: expand your thinking for bigger drops. "Thank you for stepping in front of the trend, because somebody has to do it." LH/LL is a downtrend even when "anyone and their grandmother" calls it sideways. Stay a trend trader until it chops up enough to justify a range mindset.

Bottom buying is mostly fruitless

Many bought BTC's ~15k capitulation. Few held through two months of 10% chop. Probing against the trend (Soros) pays only with 10+ years of experience. In a downtrend he is "just chilling on the sideline": the same method that adapts will catch the next trend, so there is no urgency to catch a 25% bounce.

If you must: very-HTF inflection, small, laddered

To justify buying a downtrend, zoom far out: BTC's 200D + the 52k demand zone while the HTF is still up ("still in a bull market"). But it sat ~25% below the 4H 200s loss, and alts already down 50% could get destroyed. "At least acknowledge that you are catching a knife." If you step in front of the train, skew execution: small size, laddered bids.

DCA belongs at support

Support is fine for knife-catching and for DCA. If you are DCA-ing BTC over 5 years, "it probably makes sense to do that at a support level." That is investor Mercury. Trader Mercury's main account ignores support bounces, because they are not where he makes the bulk of his money.

On review day, label each holding on the ladder: all up → hold, pre-place dip bids at the 4H/daily 200s. Local lost → expect the HTF test and bid there. HTF lost + daily LL → downtrend: cancel dip bids and sit out. Only if the weekly is still up, place 2–3 small laddered limit bids inside the 200D + demand zone. Investor DCA orders sit at support and don't depend on any of this.

By May 2024: don't rely on the HTF bull market being maintained, "for every bull market there's a bear market" ▶ Bottom Buying 23:17. Both are reconciled by the same exit: 4H 200s + daily structure.

The anchor varies. 4H 200s for BTC/SOL, 2H 200s for ETH in 2022 ▶ TA vs Fund. 9:06, 200D for HTF inflections: "whatever the trend is respecting".

If → then

  • All TFs up → hold and add on dips along the local trend.
  • Local trend (1H 200s) lost → expect a pullback to the 4H 200s. Don't call the top.
  • 4H 200s lost + daily LL → downtrend: stop buying dips, sit out.
  • Want to buy a downtrend → only at a very-HTF inflection (200D + demand) with the weekly still up; small, laddered, and admit it's a knife.
  • DCA over years → at support levels.
