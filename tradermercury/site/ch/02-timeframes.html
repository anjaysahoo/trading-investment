Timeframes: 200s cloud, snowball → snowman, scaling

After this: pick the 200s timeframe a chart respects, and know which bigger trend it stands in for.

The 200s cloud

His whole trend tool is the 200 MA plus the 200 EMA on one timeframe, his "blue and green lines" ("the 200s"). When he asked other traders whether to use the MA or the EMA, the answer split 50/50, so he uses both. Two lines make a general region rather than a single line, and that stops your brain from hunting for a pico top or bottom.

Snowflake → snowball → snowman

"Every snowman starts as a big snowball… every tiny snowball starts as a snowflake." A trend starts on the LTF and builds up. A 1–15m move is a snowflake, "just noise". 30m–2H is a snowball, 4H–12H a bigger snowball, and the snowman is every timeframe up to the daily/weekly saying the same thing. Don't call tops or bottoms before a real snowball has formed.

Weakness builds the same way

A breakdown mirrors a breakout. It starts as a snowflake, then comes a daily LH, then a daily LL, and that's a full bearish snowman. Only when strength is lost on every timeframe can you say "in hindsight… probably the macro top". A big bearish LTF snowball doesn't melt the HTF bullish snowman: it's possible to be "HTF bullish, LTF bearish" at the same time.

Ask the chart what it respects

"Not gonna force my system down Bitcoin's throat." Use the 200s timeframe that the asset has historically and recently respected, i.e. its "line of best fit". If price crosses back and forth through it, there's no edge on that timeframe, so swap to a more local tier. Each asset has a local, an HTF and a very-HTF tier.

Scaling assumptions

"Weekly still uptrending" can mean price drops −33% and you're still "bullish". He calls that "shitty analysis". If every LTF trend is bearish, he doesn't care about the very-HTF trend. Scale down to the nearest trend that is actually close to price.

The white-space ladder

Reclaim the intraday trend and you can assume a test of the daily trend. The gap in between (~11–12% in his example) is white space. A reclaim of the 2H 200s is the snowball, the 4H 200s is the body of the snowman, and then comes the 200D. Take it level to level. To get back down to the 4H 200s, price first has to lose the 1H, and before that the 15m.

Bounces aren't strength. Wicks aren't strength.

"You do not get a cookie" for a +7% bounce under the trend. "You're making cash, I'm printing wealth." What counts is an HH on a meaningful timeframe, like the "first higher high on the 4H since March 27th". Candlestick patterns only reinforce an idea, and every textbook candle has 4–5 counterexamples.

On a bi-weekly review you can't act on 15m/30m tiers, so treat them as not-biweekly. Start your ladder at the 4H 200s (daily trend) and the 200D (weekly trend). Every two weeks, write down which 200s each asset is respecting and where the next band above sits. That band is your next target.

If → then

  • Price crosses a 200s back and forth → that timeframe isn't respected. Use a more local one.
  • The HTF thesis needs a 30%+ move to be invalidated → not actionable. Drop to the next-lower proxy.
  • An LTF trend is reclaimed → assume a test of the next-higher 200s (the white space).
  • The LTF bullish arguments are lost → assume a pullback to the next support still standing.
  • A bounce or wick under the trend → ignore it. Wait for an HH on a meaningful timeframe.
